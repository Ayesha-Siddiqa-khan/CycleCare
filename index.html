    Diet & Nutrition

    General wellness guidance only — not medical or dietetic advice. Needs vary from person to person.

    During Your Period

    • Stay hydrated — drink water regularly through the day.
    • Eat balanced meals at regular times, according to your personal needs.
    • Include iron-containing foods, since iron is lost with menstrual blood.
    • Include protein-rich foods.
    • Include a variety of fruits and vegetables.
    • Include fiber-rich foods.
    • Choose nutritious sources of calcium and other essential nutrients.

    Foods to Include

    • Leafy green vegetables
    • Lentils and beans
    • Eggs
    • Fish or other protein sources
    • Yogurt or other calcium-containing foods
    • Fruits
    • Nuts and seeds
    • Whole grains
    • Iron-containing foods

    Foods to Limit

    These are general suggestions, not strict rules. No food is universally forbidden — notice how foods affect how you feel and adjust gently.

    • Excessively salty foods, if they worsen bloating.
    • Excessive caffeine, if it worsens your symptoms.
    • Highly processed foods when they replace balanced meals.
    • Excessive sugary foods.

    Precautions & Self-Care

    General self-care guidance. What helps varies from person to person and from cycle to cycle.

    Everyday Self-Care

    • Staying hydrated.
    • Getting adequate rest.
    • Gentle physical activity when comfortable.
    • Maintaining personal hygiene.
    • Using suitable menstrual hygiene products.
    • Changing menstrual products according to the manufacturer's instructions and normal hygiene practices.
    • Using warmth for comfort if helpful.
    • Monitoring your symptoms and noting changes from your normal.

    When to Seek Medical Advice

    CycleCare cannot diagnose anything. A qualified healthcare professional may be appropriate if you experience:

    • Very heavy or unusual bleeding.
    • Severe or worsening pain.
    • Fainting or significant weakness.
    • Fever or signs of infection.
    • Bleeding between periods that is unusual for you.
    • A significant change from your normal cycle.
    • Symptoms that persist or interfere substantially with daily activities.

    If you are concerned about your symptoms, please seek professional medical advice.
