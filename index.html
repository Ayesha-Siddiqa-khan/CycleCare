    Diet & Nutrition

    Nutritional guidance focused on replenishing lost minerals, easing muscle cramps, and stabilizing energy during menstruation.

    Iron & Energy Replenishment

    Iron is lost with menstrual blood. Pairing iron-rich foods with Vitamin C improves absorption:

    • Leafy Greens: Spinach, kale, and broccoli.
    • Lentils & Beans: Excellent plant-based iron and sustained energy.
    • Proteins: Eggs, lean poultry, fish, and tofu to prevent sluggishness.
    • Dark Chocolate: High in magnesium and iron to help soothe cramps.

    Hydration & Bloating Relief

    Proper hydration flushes sodium and actually reduces hormonal water retention:

    • Warm Water & Infusions: Promotes blood flow and relaxes abdominal muscles.
    • Herbal Teas: Chamomile, ginger, and peppermint soothe digestion and spasms.
    • Water-Rich Fruits: Watermelon, cucumber, berries, and oranges.
    • Fiber-Rich Grains: Oats and quinoa support gentle digestion and regulate blood sugar.

    Foods to Moderate (Symptom Triggers)

    These are general sensitivities rather than forbidden foods. Notice how your body reacts:

    • High-Sodium Foods: Canned goods, chips, and processed snacks worsen water retention.
    • Excessive Caffeine: Can constrict blood vessels and amplify uterine cramping.
    • Refined Sugars: Cause rapid energy spikes followed by mood and fatigue crashes.
    • Fried or Heavy Meals: May increase gastrointestinal discomfort or nausea.

    Precautions & Self-Care

    Practical personal care, hygiene safety protocols, physical comfort measures, and medical warning signs.

    Menstrual Hygiene & Product Safety

    Essential practices to prevent bacterial growth and irritation:

    • Regular Changes: Change sanitary pads every 4–6 hours, tampons every 4–8 hours max.
    • Menstrual Cups: Empty every 8–12 hours; sterilize in boiling water between cycles.
    • Gentle Hygiene: Cleanse the external area with warm water; avoid scented washes or douching.
    • Breathable Fabrics: Wear loose, breathable cotton underwear for comfort and air flow.

    Comfort & Cramp Management

    Non-medicinal methods to relieve tension and soothe pelvic cramps:

    • Heat Therapy: Apply a heating pad or hot water bottle to the lower abdomen or lower back.
    • Gentle Movement: Light walking, yoga poses (Child's Pose, Cat-Cow), and pelvic tilts.
    • Adequate Sleep: Prioritize 7–9 hours; side-sleeping with a pillow between knees reduces spinal pressure.
    • Warm Baths: Relax tight muscles and reduce emotional stress.

    When to Seek Professional Medical Care

    CycleCare is for tracking only and cannot diagnose. Consult a qualified doctor or gynecologist if you notice:

    • Excessive Bleeding: Soaking through one or more pads/tampons every hour for two consecutive hours.
    • Debilitating Pain: Severe cramps that do not respond to usual relief or prevent normal daily activities.
    • Irregular Spotting: Unexplained bleeding or spotting between periods or after intercourse.
    • Infection Symptoms: Fever, chills, unusual or foul-smelling vaginal discharge.
    • Fainting or Extreme Weakness: Dizziness or pallor, which can indicate acute blood loss or anemia.
